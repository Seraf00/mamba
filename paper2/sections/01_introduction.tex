\section{Introduction}

Two-dimensional echocardiography is the first-line modality for assessing
cardiac function, and delineation of the left-ventricular endocardium,
myocardium and left atrium underpins every downstream measurement, ejection
fraction chief among them. The CAMUS benchmark~\cite{leclerc2019camus} has been
occupied since its release by encoder--decoder
networks~\cite{ronneberger2015unet,isensee2021nnunet} and transformer hybrids
such as TransUNet~\cite{chen2021transunet} and Swin-UNet~\cite{cao2022swinunet}.

That family is a poor fit for where echocardiography is heading. Handheld
probes push acquisition to the bedside, where inference must run on an embedded
accelerator alongside a live image stream. The YOLO family~\cite{redmon2016yolo}
targets that operating point, with a segmentation head derived from YOLACT's
prototype-mask formulation~\cite{bolya2019yolact}.

Applying one to CAMUS is not a matter of changing the dataloader. A YOLO
instance mask is supervised as a \emph{single closed polygon}, and one of the
three CAMUS structures -- the myocardium -- is an annulus. A polygon has no
hole. It is natural to assume that how one resolves this matters. We show it
does not, and nor does almost any other configuration choice.

\textbf{An encoding, and its ceiling.} We represent the epicardium as a
\emph{filled} contour and recover the myocardium by subtraction, keeping the
mask head on simply-connected shapes (Sec.~\ref{sec:encoding}). We quantify what
such an encoding can express as an explicit \emph{ceiling}: at 96 vertices the
recomposed label map reaches $0.997$ Dice and $0.308$\,mm HD95 -- exactly one
pixel. The polygon representation is not what limits a detector here. We further
show the ceiling is a valid bound but \emph{not} predictive: where it forecasts
a resolvable gap between encodings, none appears.

\textbf{A study reported against a measured noise floor.} We sweep encoding,
vertex budget, scale, generation, resolution, mask-prototype resolution,
augmentation, pretraining, data scale, loss weighting and optimiser on one
laptop GPU (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). Critically we first measure run-to-run
variance: three seeds of one configuration span $0.134$\,mm HD95, which a paired
test over the 200 test frames calls significant ($p{=}0.035$). Read against that
floor, \emph{no} configuration change improves on the stock one; what matters is
training-side, while every capacity knob is null or harmful.

\textbf{A like-for-like comparison.} We evaluate on the official CAMUS split,
verified byte-identical to the released subgroup files, against nine baselines
under one protocol, with HD95 at native resolution and one latency protocol for
every model (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). A 2.7\,M-parameter detector matches or
exceeds all of them, exceeds published inter-observer agreement, and is
near-invariant to input resolution where encoder--decoders are not.
