\section{Conclusion}

We asked how close a real-time instance detector gets to encoder--decoder
networks on multi-structure echocardiography, and what stands in the way. The
apparent obstacle is representational: the CAMUS myocardium is an annulus and a
YOLO mask is a polygon. Encoding the epicardium as a filled contour and
recovering the myocardium by subtraction resolves it at a cost of one pixel of
Hausdorff distance.

The obstacle then turns out not to be one. Neither the encoding nor the vertex
budget produces a measurable difference, and neither does scale, resolution,
mask-prototype resolution, augmentation recipe, training-set size or a
boundary-weighted loss. Read against a measured seed floor of $0.134$\,mm, a
stock YOLO26n-seg at 640\,px matches or exceeds nine tuned baselines at 2.7\,M
parameters and runs at near-constant cost across input resolutions where
encoder--decoders scale with pixel count.

Two findings generalise beyond this architecture. Mean Dice on CAMUS has
saturated at the level of annotator disagreement, so boundary distance in
millimetres is what discriminates methods. And a paired test over the 200-frame
test set certifies pure seed variation as significant, so single-seed ablations
on this benchmark produce false positives.

Because in-distribution accuracy appears annotation-bound, the open question is
generalisation across scanners and centres rather than further gains here.
