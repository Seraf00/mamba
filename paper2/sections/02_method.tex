\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Data and splits}

CAMUS~\cite{leclerc2019camus} provides apical two- and four-chamber views for
500 patients, with expert delineations of the LV endocardium, LV myocardium and
left atrium at end-diastole (ED) and end-systole (ES). We use the official
400/50/50 split, verified byte-identical to the \texttt{subgroup} lists released
with the dataset, giving $1600$ training and $200$ test frames. Each patient also
carries a fully annotated half cardiac cycle, used as a data-scaling ablation
($17$k frames). Images are exported at native resolution with per-image
min--max normalisation; no resampling to a common grid occurs, so the native
pixel spacing (typically $0.308$\,mm) remains valid for boundary metrics.

\subsection{The annulus problem}
\label{sec:encoding}

CAMUS labels form a 4-class map: background, endocardium ($\ell{=}1$),
myocardium ($\ell{=}2$) and atrium ($\ell{=}3$). The myocardium is the ring
between the endocardial and epicardial contours -- topologically an annulus, and
so not representable as the single closed polygon a YOLO label must be.

\textbf{Filled-contour decomposition (ours).} We define the second class as the
\emph{epicardial disc} $\ell\in\{1,2\}$, which is simply connected, so all three
trained classes are blob-shaped. At inference the CAMUS map is recomposed by
painting the predicted disc as myocardium and overwriting its interior with the
predicted endocardium:
\begin{equation}
\hat{M}_{\text{myo}} \;=\; \hat{M}_{\text{epi}} \setminus \hat{M}_{\text{endo}} .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The mask head never represents a hole; the hole emerges from two predictions.

\textbf{Seam-cut annulus.} Alternatively the ring is written as one polygon
traversing the outer contour, crossing to the inner along a zero-width seam, and
returning in opposite winding. Scan-line filling -- including the routine
Ultralytics uses -- honours this and yields a true annulus, but the mask head
must learn a shape whose interior is excluded. Taking only the \emph{outer}
contour is not a third option: it \emph{is} the epicardial contour, hence
identical to the filled decomposition.

Both encodings are lossy at finite vertex budgets, so we measure their ceiling
directly: ground-truth maps are converted to polygons, rasterised back, and
scored against the untouched native ground truth.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

To keep our numbers comparable with published baselines on this split we
reproduce their protocol rather than improve on it. Overlap metrics are computed
per frame and class on a $256\times256$ grid; boundary metrics (HD95, ASSD) at
\emph{native} resolution with each patient's true pixel spacing, in millimetres
-- computing the latter on a resampled grid under-reports distances by the
resampling factor. Predicted masks are resampled to native resolution by nearest
neighbour, keeping the highest-confidence instance per class. We verified this
reimplementation against the reference metric code: HD95 agrees to
$2\times10^{-12}$\,mm and ASSD exactly.

\subsection{Training configuration}

Unless stated otherwise we train YOLO26n-seg from COCO weights at $640$\,px,
batch 8, 100 epochs with early stopping, on one NVIDIA RTX~4060 Laptop GPU
(8\,GB). YOLO26 is the newest generation with published segmentation weights and
is \emph{end-to-end}: it predicts a fixed instance set with no non-maximum
suppression, removing a data-dependent stage from the latency budget. We use
Ultralytics' automatic optimiser selection, which chooses MuSGD at lr $0.01$
here and overrides any user learning rate; learning rate is therefore swept only
in the dedicated arm of Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}.

Several components of the stock augmentation have no anatomical justification on
sector ultrasound: mosaic composites sectors into images that cannot occur,
horizontal flipping violates the left/right convention of an apical view, and
hue/saturation jitter is meaningless on grayscale. We adopt an \emph{echo-aware}
default -- mosaic, mixup, copy-paste and flips off; mild affine and brightness
jitter retained -- chosen on principle and cost, \emph{not} because it measures
better: it is statistically indistinguishable from the stock recipe.
