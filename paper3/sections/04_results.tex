\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{How large an effect is measurable?}
\label{sec:noise}

We begin with the noise floor, because it determines how everything that follows
may be read. Table~\ref{tab:seeds} reports three trainings of one configuration
differing only in random seed.

\input{tables/Y7_seedfloor}

Two consequences follow. First, HD95 varies by $0.134$\,mm from the seed alone,
so no smaller difference should be attributed to a design choice; we adopt this
as the threshold throughout. Second, and more troubling for evaluation practice
generally, a paired Wilcoxon test between seeds 0 and 1 --- runs identical in
every respect except the random seed --- returns $p{=}0.035$ on HD95. A paired
test over 200 frames is powerful enough to certify pure seed variation as a
significant effect. Reporting $p<0.05$ from single-seed runs on this benchmark
is therefore not sufficient evidence of an improvement.

The most seed-sensitive quantity is not an accuracy metric at all but the number
of frames on which a structure is missed entirely, which ranges from 1 to 6
across these three runs. We return to this in Sec.~\ref{sec:detection}.

\subsection{Encoding ceiling}

Table~\ref{tab:ceiling} reports what each encoding can express as a function of
the vertex budget $n$.

\input{tables/Y3_ceiling}

The filled decomposition is effectively lossless at a modest budget: at $n{=}96$
it reaches $0.9970$ Dice and $0.308$\,mm HD95. Since the CAMUS pixel spacing is
$0.308$\,mm, that HD95 is exactly one pixel --- the discretisation floor. The
polygon representation is therefore not the limiting factor for a detector on
this task, which is the premise the remainder of the study rests on.

The two encodings are nearly indistinguishable on the recomposed label map,
because Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} re-carves the hole either way. They differ on the
raw second-class target: at $n{=}32$ the filled disc is reproduced at $0.9953$
Dice against $0.9671$ for the seam-cut annulus, a gap of $0.028$. A thin ring
must spend its vertex budget on two contours rather than one, so each is
described half as finely.

\subsection{Thirteen factors, no improvements}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} collects the ablation grid. Read against the
$0.134$\,mm floor, the summary is quickly stated: nothing improves on the stock
configuration.

\input{tables/Y2_ablation}

\paragraph{Overlap is saturated; boundary distance is not} Across the $32$
distinct configurations trained in this study, Dice spans $0.9035$ to $0.9177$
and HD95 spans $3.70$ to $4.07$\,mm. Stated as ranges these look comparable, but
a range grows with the number of samples drawn and so cannot be set against the
three-seed floor of Sec.~\ref{sec:noise}. The standard deviation can, because it
is not biased by sample size, and on that statistic the two axes separate
cleanly. Between-configuration Dice varies \emph{less} than the seed floor
($0.0037$ against $0.0045$, a ratio of $0.83$), while between-configuration HD95
varies half again as much ($0.099$ against $0.068$, a ratio of $1.46$).

Thirteen design factors therefore move Dice by no more than retraining one
configuration under a different seed, and move boundary distance measurably
more. This is the central negative result of the paper, and it is stronger than
a range comparison would suggest: it is not that the factors fail to clear a
threshold, but that on overlap they produce less spread than doing nothing at
all. Reporting Dice alone on CAMUS therefore conceals most of what an ablation
reveals, and is the single clearest argument for the millimetre-scale
native-resolution protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\paragraph{Capacity does not help} Model scale from $2.7$ to $27$\,M parameters,
input resolution from $512$ to $960$\,px, and mask-prototype resolution all fail
to beat the noise floor. Full-resolution prototypes are significantly
\emph{worse} ($+0.157$\,mm, $p{=}0.008$) at $3.5\times$ the training cost, which
is consistent with over-parameterising the mask path on 1600 images. Input
resolution above $640$\,px is flat: giving the mask head $240\times240$
prototypes instead of $160\times160$ changes nothing.

\paragraph{Nor does more data} Training on the full annotated cardiac cycle ---
$17{,}006$ frames, $10.6\times$ the supervision --- does not improve ED/ES
accuracy. The additional frames interpolate between the two extremes they are
meant to support and are highly correlated with them, so they add little
independent information.

\paragraph{Training-side factors do register} Removing augmentation entirely
costs $0.318$\,mm ($p{<}0.001$), the largest effect measured anywhere in this
study --- larger than $10\times$ the parameters, larger than any resolution
change. COCO initialisation is worth $0.183$\,mm ($p{=}0.009$) despite grayscale
sector ultrasound resembling nothing in COCO, which suggests the transfer is of
low-level edge and texture filters that 1600 images cannot supply.

The \emph{recipe}, however, does not matter: our anatomically-motivated
augmentation is statistically indistinguishable from the stock natural-image
recipe ($p{=}0.31$), even though the latter includes mosaic compositing and
horizontal flipping that we argued are anatomically invalid. Adding horizontal
flipping alone to the echo-aware recipe is harmful ($+0.203$\,mm, $p{=}0.019$),
yet the stock recipe --- which also flips --- is not. We did not isolate this
interaction and report it as unresolved.

\paragraph{Neither the objective nor the optimiser} A boundary-weighted mask
loss, which upweights pixels near the contour by a factor $1+\lambda$, fails at
every $\lambda \in \{2,5,10\}$ and at its mildest setting is significantly
harmful ($+0.216$\,mm, $p{<}0.001$). Scaling the segmentation, classification or
distribution-focal loss gains, and substituting AdamW or SGD for the
automatically selected optimiser, leave HD95 inside the noise floor in all six
arms ($p \geq 0.06$). Doubling the weight on the segmentation loss --- the term
most directly tied to boundary quality --- changes HD95 by $0.001$\,mm.

\subsection{The annulus is a non-problem, and the ceiling is not predictive}

Neither encoding nor vertex budget produces a measurable difference: filled
versus seam at 96 vertices ($p{=}0.87$), 96 versus 32 vertices ($p{=}0.31$), and
filled versus seam at 32 vertices ($p{=}0.34$).

The last of these is a genuine falsification and worth stating plainly. The
ceiling analysis predicts a $0.228$\,mm penalty for the seam encoding at 32
vertices. That exceeds the noise floor and should have been visible. The
observed difference is $0.019$\,mm, with the opposite sign. \emph{The encoding
ceiling is a valid upper bound but not a predictor of trained-model
differences}: a representational penalty of $0.2$\,mm is simply invisible when
model error is $3.7$\,mm. Practically, any reasonable encoding suffices, and we
recommend the filled decomposition because it is simpler to implement --- no
seam construction, no winding-order bugs --- not because it scores better.

\subsection{Comparison with semantic segmentation}

\input{tables/Y1_main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} places the configuration alongside nine baselines on the
official split. Averaged over three seeds it reaches $0.9113$ mean Dice and
$3.809$\,mm HD95 at $2.7$\,M parameters, against $0.9121$/$4.081$\,mm for
TransUNet ($102$\,M) and $0.9099$/$4.270$\,mm for nnU-Net ($15.6$\,M).

The two axes do not give the same answer, and the difference is the point of
this paper. On boundary distance the detector is the best in the benchmark by
$0.271$\,mm, which is twice the $0.134$\,mm seed floor of
Sec.~\ref{sec:noise} and therefore a difference we are willing to attribute. On
overlap it is not distinguishable from the leading baselines: tested over the
$50$ patients rather than the $200$ frames, with seeds averaged rather than the
best selected, it separates from five of the nine baselines and not from
TransUNet, nnU-Net, UNet-V1 or UNet-ResNet --- and its point estimate is
$0.0008$ Dice \emph{behind} TransUNet.

We report it this way because the alternative is available and flattering:
scoring seed~0 over the $200$ frames yields $0.9151$ Dice and separation from
eight of nine baselines. A paper arguing that single-seed frame-level tests
overstate differences cannot then rest its own headline on one.

Two external comparisons are available on the same official split. Against a
contour-guided boundary-aware method reporting per-structure Dice of
$93.95$/$88.09$/$89.15$ (mean $90.40$), our seed mean is
$93.86$/$87.91$/$91.62$ (mean $91.13$): ahead on the left atrium and on the
three-class mean, and marginally behind on both ventricular classes. Reporting
our best seed instead would have given $94.19$/$88.60$/$91.74$ and a clean sweep,
which is precisely the selection this paper argues against. Against a multi-task
framework reporting $0.951$ Dice for the LV cavity at ED, our LV-endocardial
Dice at ED is $0.9523$.

We attach limited weight to either, and note that both are additionally
compromised as comparisons: the published figures are single runs, ours are seed
means, and neither paper reports the variance that would make the difference
interpretable. All the gaps here are of the order of our own seed floor. The
more informative reference point is the dataset authors' own: average
inter-observer Dice on CAMUS is $0.899$. Every model in
Table~\ref{tab:main} above $0.90$ is already operating beyond the level at which
two cardiologists agree.

\subsection{Latency}

\input{tables/Y5_latency}

Under a single protocol the detector is \emph{near-invariant to input
resolution}: $15.7$\,ms at $256$\,px and $15.5$\,ms at $640$\,px, a $4\%$
increase for $6.25\times$ the pixels. Every encoder--decoder scales with pixel
count over the same range, by factors of four to eight. At matched $640$\,px the
detector is between $1.5\times$ and $23\times$ faster than every baseline;
end-to-end inference including pre-processing and native-resolution mask
assembly is $11.8$\,ms.

This is a property of the architecture family rather than a tuning outcome: the
detector's cost is dominated by fixed per-layer overhead rather than by
convolution over pixels at this scale, so the resolution one would want for
boundary accuracy is nearly free, where for an encoder--decoder it is punitive.

Neither FP16 ($16.7$\,ms) nor ONNX Runtime export ($23.5$\,ms) improved on the
PyTorch path. At $2.7$\,M parameters the conversion and transfer overheads
dominate. We report this because the opposite is commonly assumed.

\subsection{Ejection fraction}
\label{sec:ef}

\input{tables/Y6_ef}

Computed with the official implementation at native resolution, the detector
reaches $9.24\%$ MAE at $r{=}0.756$: within the range spanned by the baselines
($6.66$--$9.74\%$, $r{=}0.640$--$0.827$) but not leading them. We claim no EF
advantage.

The ceiling is more informative than the ranking. Feeding the \emph{ground-truth}
masks through the identical pipeline yields $5.44\%$ MAE at $r{=}0.863$. That is
the irreducible gap between mask-derived EF and the clinically recorded value,
and it consumes the majority of every model's error. EF on CAMUS is therefore
substantially method-limited rather than segmentation-limited.

\paragraph{A fault in the reference implementation} The official Simpson code
selects \texttt{find\_contours($\cdot$)[0]} --- the first contour returned,
ordered by position rather than by area. Ground truth is single-component, so
this never manifests and the fault has survived in released code. Model
predictions carry occasional spurious blobs, and because EF requires all four of
a patient's masks, a handful of such frames poisons whole patients.

We quantified this by re-running all nine baselines with the filter disabled.
The effect is severe where it occurs and absent elsewhere. On UNet-V1, EF
correlation collapses from $r{=}0.825$ to $r{=}0.080$ and MAE rises from
$6.62\%$ to $11.34\%$ purely by removing the filter; DenseContextU-Net moves
from $9.38\%$ to $12.88\%$ ($r$ $0.763$ to $0.550$). The remaining seven
baselines are unchanged to the precision we report. Whether the fault bites
therefore depends on whether a given model emits spurious components at all,
which is not a property one can assume --- and a pipeline that never checks will
not distinguish the two cases.

We verified that this is specific to native-resolution evaluation. On a
$256\times256$ grid the spurious components are sub-pixel and vanish, and
filtering slightly \emph{degrades} the result. Practitioners computing EF from
predictions at native resolution must apply a connected-component filter; those
working on a resampled grid are protected by accident rather than by design.

\subsection{Detection reliability is a separate axis}
\label{sec:detection}

A detector can decline to emit an instance, leaving a structure with no contour
at all --- a failure mode a semantic network cannot have, since it assigns every
pixel a label. This is invisible to Dice computed over detected frames and
dominates Dice computed over all frames. In one comparison an apparent $0.0116$
Dice deficit was traced entirely to nine dropped instances; on the 190 frames
where both models detected all three structures they were statistically
identical ($p{=}0.72$).

It is also the most seed-sensitive quantity we measured (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}),
which means single-run miss counts should not be read as properties of a
configuration.

Crucially it is eliminable. Lowering the confidence threshold from the
natural-image default of $0.25$ to $0.10$ removes every missed detection --- 0
of 200 frames --- while leaving Dice and HD95 unchanged. Where every structure
is known a priori to be present, the usual precision--recall trade-off does not
apply, and the threshold should be set far below the detection default.
