\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to determine what still improves boundary accuracy on a cardiac
ultrasound benchmark whose overlap metric has reached the level of annotator
agreement, using real-time instance segmentation as the test bed.

The representational obstacle we expected to matter does not. The CAMUS
myocardium is an annulus and an instance mask is a polygon, but encoding the
epicardium as a filled contour and recovering the myocardium by subtraction
costs one pixel of Hausdorff distance, and no encoding or vertex budget we
tested produces a measurable difference in a trained model. The encoding ceiling
is a valid upper bound; it is not a predictor.

Nor does anything else. Across thirteen factors and roughly fifty training runs
--- capacity, generation, input resolution, mask-prototype resolution,
augmentation recipe, pretraining, data scale, a boundary-weighted loss, the
three loss gains and the optimiser --- no configuration change improves on a
stock setting once run-to-run variance is measured. The two factors that do
register, augmentation and initialisation, both concern the information
available to the model rather than its capacity. The resulting configuration
reaches $0.9113$ seed-mean Dice and $3.809$\,mm HD95 at $2.7$\,M parameters:
the best boundary accuracy in the benchmark, with overlap indistinguishable from
the leading baselines. It is near-invariant to input resolution where
encoder--decoders scale with pixel count.

Three findings generalise beyond this architecture. Overlap has saturated on
CAMUS, so boundary distance in millimetres is what discriminates methods and
reporting Dice alone conceals most of what an ablation reveals. A paired test
over a 200-frame test set certifies pure seed variation as significant, so
single-seed ablations on benchmarks of this size produce false positives. And
the reference implementation for CAMUS ejection fraction selects a contour by
position rather than area --- harmless on ground truth, ruinous on predictions.

Taken together these point to the annotations as the operative limit on
in-distribution accuracy, supported by three converging observations: mean Dice
already exceeds inter-observer agreement, weighting the loss toward the contour
is actively harmful, and perfect segmentation still leaves most of the ejection
fraction error intact. If that reading is right, the productive questions are no
longer about architecture on this dataset. They are about generalisation across
scanners and centres, about temporal consistency across the cardiac cycle --- a
supervision signal a per-frame model discards --- and about whether the
benchmark's reference standard can support the precision that current methods
are already reporting against it.
